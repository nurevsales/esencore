Why We Do What We Do

The TrustProblem.

Almost everything sold as a research peptide arrives with a number attached to it. Very little of it arrives with evidence. This page is about the difference, and about what we decided to do about it.

Esencore  /  Position Paper Research Use Only
01  /  The Claim

A Number IsNot A Result.

99% purity. 7x tested. These read like findings. They are not. A purity figure means something only when you can say what was measured, by which method, on which batch, on what date, and by whom. Detached from those five things it is a sentence on a webpage.

The multiplier is the clearest example, because it does not describe a test at all. It describes a count nobody defines — seven of what, against which specification, on how many batches, by how many laboratories. And then watch which direction it moves.

  1. 3x
  2. 4x
  3. 7x
  4. 15xNow

Same phrase — no defined unit

Some of that climb is real. A certificate covering identity, purity, assay, metals and sterility genuinely does more than one covering solvents alone. But nobody defines the unit, so the number cannot tell you which kind of increase you are looking at: a broader scope, or just a bigger number. Only one of those costs anything to produce.

Then the material moves. A TikTok recommendation, a Facebook group, a WhatsApp channel, someone reliable two towns over. Each hand-off copies the claim forward intact, because a claim is far easier to copy than a test is to run. By the time it reaches a buyer, the number has travelled much further than any evidence behind it ever did.

The number arrives. The instrument run that produced it usually does not.

02  /  The Certificate

A COA DescribesOne Vial.

A certificate of analysis is a record of what an instrument saw in the specific sample placed in front of it. That is exactly what makes it valuable, and exactly what limits it. It is evidence about one vial, from one batch, on one day. It is not a property of the shipment it gets stapled to.

Watch what that means in practice. A hundred boxes arrive under a single claim and a single certificate. You pull a vial from box one and send it out. It comes back clean. Nothing about that result reaches box eight.

So when box eight comes back at half the stated purity, the first result does not become wrong — it simply stops being relevant. You are now holding ninety-eight boxes in an unknown state, and one certificate that never described them. The material has not changed. Your knowledge of it has collapsed.

One good result does not travel across a shipment. One bad result travels across all of it.

03  /  The Arithmetic

Checking It CostsMore Than Buying It.

The obvious answer is to test it yourself. The reason almost nobody does is not negligence. It is the bill.

  • 100 Boxes in an order of the size a small brand actually places.
  • $150+ Typical minimum to run a single vial at an outside laboratory.
  • $15,000 To put one vial from every box through that laboratory.

One vial per box — and that is the cheap version

It still tells you nothing about the other vials inside the box it came from. Real confidence means testing every vial, which costs more than the inventory is worth and destroys the inventory to find out. So the testing does not happen, the claim goes unchecked, and the entire market runs on a single assumption: that somebody upstream already did the work.

That assumption is the product most of this industry is actually selling.

04  /  The Questions

What TestingActually Answers.

“Tested” is not one thing. A buyer sending material to a laboratory is asking specific, separate questions, and a certificate answers only the ones that were actually run.

  • Identity

    Is the compound in this vial the compound named on the label — and is it the only one present?

  • Purity

    What proportion of the material is the named compound, and what makes up the remainder?

  • Content

    How much compound is physically in the vial, measured against what the label states?

  • Endotoxin

    What bacterial endotoxin load does the material carry, expressed against a defined limit?

A vial that fails on identity is a different problem from one that passes identity and fails on content. A certificate that reports a purity figure and nothing else has answered one question out of four — and it is not the question that catches material which was never the right compound to begin with.

Now hold two real certificates side by side. One is labelled 8x: purity, net content, identity by LC-MS, endotoxin, microbial growth and four heavy metals, reported across four vials. The other is labelled 15x and runs to three pages: identity, chromatographic purity, assay vial by vial, heavy metals by ICP-MS, sterility, endotoxin, a sixteen-solvent residual panel, container closure integrity, solubility, TFA, and a fentanyl screen.

Both are real work, competently done, by accredited laboratories. And the numbers are useless for choosing between them, because two different laboratories counted two different things. An 8x is not smaller than a 15x. It is not measured in the same unit — there is no unit.

You cannot compare two multipliers. Nobody has defined the x.

05  /  Coverage

An Independent LabAnswers One Question.

Give independence its due first. A certificate from an unaffiliated accredited laboratory settles whether the person running the instrument had any reason to shade the result. That is worth having. It is also something we do not have, and we say so further down this page.

But it answers a question almost nobody was actually worried about. Nobody lies awake wondering whether the laboratory faked the chromatogram. “Tested” bundles three separate questions together, and independence answers only the first.

  • Who ran it

    Was the analyst neutral? An outside accredited laboratory settles this completely, and a good one will tell you what it is accredited for.

  • What was run

    Identity, purity, content, endotoxin, metals, solvents — which of them? This is the scope the multiplier gestures at without ever defining.

  • How far it reaches

    How many vials, out of a lot of what size, chosen by whom — and what happened to the material before it was ever put in a vial?

A certificate answers the first two well. It cannot answer the third, and the careful laboratories say so in as many words: in the small print under the signatures, a properly written report records that the laboratory did not observe or take part in selecting the sample, and cannot confirm the sample is authentic or that it represents the lot it is associated with.

Read that twice, because it is the laboratory being scrupulous and it is this whole page in one line. Everything above it is true of the vials that arrived in the post. Nothing above it reaches the rest of the batch, or reaches backwards to where the material came from. The analysis was never the weak link. The weak link is the step between the batch and the envelope, and no instrument can see it.

Which is how a seller ends up holding a genuinely independent certificate on three vials while having no way to tell you what is in the other several hundred. If those vials were filled from material that was bought finished and never checked on the way in, the certificate is not evidence about the batch at all. It is evidence about three vials that turned out fine.

Independence tells you the analyst was honest. It does not tell you how much of the batch they saw.

None of this is about where material comes from. Serious active ingredient is made in a handful of places and every manufacturer worth talking to buys from some of them. The question is not the map. The question is how much of what arrived was actually checked before it went into something. Which makes the useful questions short, and answerable by any seller willing to answer them:

  • 01Was every incoming lot of active material tested before it was used — or a representative sample of lots?
  • 02Was it tested on the way in, on the way out, or only once at the end?
  • 03How many vials were tested, out of a lot of what size, and who chose them?
  • 04Does the lot number printed on the certificate match the lot number on the vial in your hand?

Not one of those can be answered by a certificate. Every one of them can be answered by the person selling you the material, in a sentence, if they know. Ours are next.

06  /  Our Answer

Test The Material,Not The Paperwork.

We did not solve this by writing a better claim. We moved the testing to the two points where it changes a decision — before material is committed to a formulation, and after the batch exists. Taking the four questions above in order:

  • Incoming material is tested before production

    Active material is tested when it arrives and held until that result exists. Every lot, not a sample of lots — because a lot that was never checked is exactly the box-eight problem, moved inside our own building.

  • The finished batch is tested after production

    A vial is drawn from the batch itself and tested after the batch is made. The question it answers is not what the raw material was, but what came out the other end of our process.

  • Both results attach to the batch record

    Release is made against the batch’s own documentation, and that documentation stays associated with it. A result that is not bound to a specific batch is the same detached number we started this page complaining about.

  • The sample never travels to us in the post

    A laboratory sent a vial cannot know which batch it came from — which is exactly why the careful ones say so on the certificate. We draw the sample ourselves, from the batch we made, under our own chain of custody. That is not independence. It is the other half of the problem, and it is the half a mailed-in vial cannot solve.

Disclosure — who runs the analysis

Testing is performed in-house, by Sycamore Analytical — an affiliated laboratory rather than an outside one. This is not independent third-party testing, and we will not describe it as though it were. You should weight it accordingly.

What the arrangement does buy is the thing the arithmetic above makes impossible for most buyers: testing stops being a per-sample invoice we are tempted to economise on, so it can run on every incoming lot rather than on a representative few. That is a real difference in coverage, and it is a different claim from independence.

It also buys provenance, which is the thing an outside laboratory structurally cannot give you. Independence and provenance are separate goods, and a vial in the post carries only one of them: the analyst is neutral, but they are testing whatever turned up. We have the other one. Holding both in the same result means an outside laboratory watching the sample being drawn — uncommon in this industry, and a fair thing to ask any manufacturer about, ourselves included.

So send it out. If you want a laboratory that has no relationship with us to check our work, we would rather you had that than took our word for it — ask us for material and we will supply it. A manufacturer who only looks good under its own testing is telling you something, and we would rather not be one.

07  /  The Limits

What ThisDoes Not Prove.

A page arguing that other people overstate their evidence has an obligation to be precise about its own. So, plainly:

  • 01Testing every incoming lot reduces the chance that unidentified material reaches production. It does not make it impossible, and we do not claim that it does.
  • 02Every result describes the sample it was run on. That limit is not something we escape by being the ones running it — it applies to our certificates exactly as it applies to everyone else’s.
  • 03Coverage is not independence. We test more material than a sampling approach would; an unaffiliated laboratory would carry more weight on any single result.
  • 04Nothing on this page is a regulatory status. Esencore does not hold and does not claim FDA approval, GMP or cGMP certification, ISO certification or USP compliance, and nothing here should be read as a representation of sterility or of suitability for human use.
  • 05All Esencore products are supplied for laboratory research use only. They are not for human use or consumption.

Count The Questions,Not The Rows.

The four questions above work on whatever you are being offered, by whoever is offering it. Including on us.
